Chapter 16 review · Drill

Name the Skill

The NextGen exam tests five research skills this book names. Sort each moment.

Which skill? (Quick check)

  • Noticing that the statute never defines “public accommodation” Ambiguity spotting
    • An undefined term is exactly what the exam hides for you to find.
  • Deciding that the state supreme court decision controls and the neighboring state’s only persuades Source ranking
    • Intra-jurisdiction ranking by binding effect: the whole game on “which source governs” items.
  • Identifying that two prior robberies at the gas station were the facts the appellate decision turned on Dispositive fact identification
    • The facts that, if changed, would change the outcome.
  • Writing that the provided materials do not reach the element and naming what would Gap identification
    • “The law is unclear” versus “this universe does not answer it”: name the gap rather than paper over it.
  • Committing that the materials answer the duty question and stopping Sufficiency and closure
    • A committed sufficiency call. “Probably enough” is not a call.
  • Treating the premises-liability statute as off point because it reaches physical conditions, not third-party crime Source ranking
    • Ranking includes scope: an off-point statute does not control, and recording that is a finding.
    • Deciding what a provided source does not reach is part of ranking the universe.
  • Spotting that “foreseeable” is the word the two in-state decisions leave room on Ambiguity spotting
    • The ambiguity the item was built around.
  • Comparing the client’s facts to the applied case in the written answer Dispositive fact identification
    • The second half of the skill: compare the dispositive facts to the client’s.

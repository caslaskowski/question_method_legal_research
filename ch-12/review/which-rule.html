Chapter 12 review · Drill

Which Rule?

Sort each failure by the rule it implicates. The feedback names the step in the closing-out protocol that prevents it.

Name the rule (Quick check)

  • Relying on a case overruled two years ago because nobody ran a citator Rule 1.1 (competence)
    • The ST Engineering Marine failure. The update step in the closing-out protocol prevents it.
  • Omitting a controlling decision that cuts against your client, which you knew about Rule 3.3 (candor)
    • Directly adverse controlling authority the other side has not cited must be disclosed.
  • A partner files an associate’s brief without reviewing the research behind it Rules 5.1 and 5.3 (supervision)
    • Supervisors answer for delegated research. The Research Log is what makes review possible.
  • Citing a case for a proposition it does not actually support Rule 1.1 (competence)
    • Read before you cite. The confirm-your-propositions step catches it.
  • Filing a brief with six citations a chatbot invented Rule 3.3 (candor)
    • A fabricated citation is a false statement of law to the tribunal, whatever its source. Rule 1.1 is implicated too, since verification is part of competence.
    • Candor is the rule most directly broken; competence is close behind.
  • Running only searches designed to find support for your position Rule 3.3 (candor)
    • A one-sided search is how adverse authority stays unfound, and “I didn’t look” is no answer. The final sweep includes an adverse-authority search.
  • A paralegal verifies nothing and the supervising lawyer never asks Rules 5.1 and 5.3 (supervision)
    • Non-lawyer assistance is the lawyer’s responsibility under 5.3.
  • Not researching at all beyond one old decision Rule 1.1 (competence)
    • Thoroughness and preparation reasonably necessary for the representation. The whole method is the answer.

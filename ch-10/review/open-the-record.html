Chapter 10 review · Drill

Open the Record?

The ticket reads: “the statute is ambiguous as to X, and no higher-authority source resolves it.” Sort each moment.

Justified, or stay closed? (Quick check)

  • The term is defined in the statute’s definitions section The record stays closed
    • No ambiguity, no ticket. The text answers the question.
  • The term is undefined, no court has construed it, the agency has not defined it, and two readings are genuinely plausible Open the record
    • Every higher-authority source has been checked and the ambiguity documented. This is the one justified entry.
  • The term is undefined, but your circuit construed it last year The record stays closed
    • Case law resolves it. Legislative history cannot outrank a controlling opinion.
  • A client wants to know “why Congress passed this law” for a policy memo, not a brief Open the record
    • An exploratory purpose question is a legitimate reason to read a committee report, so long as you do not mistake it for binding authority.
  • The judge who will hear the case is a committed textualist The record stays closed
    • Even with an ambiguity, check whether the court will use what you find. For a textualist court, deploy less of this chapter’s machinery, or none.
    • The textualism check comes before the search: a court committed to plain meaning may never reach your findings.
  • The statute is ambiguous, and the agency’s regulation defines the term The record stays closed
    • The regulation is a higher-authority source than the record. Read it first; legislative history is the last stop, not the next one.

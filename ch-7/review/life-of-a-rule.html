Chapter 7 review · Drill

The Life of a Rule

Arrange the stages of a federal regulation’s life, and the document each stage leaves behind.

From delegation to currency check (Put it in order)

Top is first.

  1. Congress passes a statute delegating authority to the agency
    • The regulation is valid only within the scope of this grant, a point that is frequently litigated. The CFR part’s authority note cites it.
  2. The agency publishes a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking in the Federal Register
    • The NPRM shows the original intent and the proposed text.
  3. Stakeholders file comments during the comment period
    • The comments show what was objected to; the final rule must respond.
  4. The agency publishes the final rule with its preamble
    • The preamble explains the agency’s choices. Often the single best interpretive resource.
  5. The rule is codified in the CFR, with authority and source notes
    • The source note cites the Federal Register document that created or last amended the part: the thread you pull to reach the preamble.
  6. You verify currency in the eCFR, the LSA, the Federal Register, and a citator
    • Agencies revise rules far more often than legislatures amend statutes; currency checking matters more here.

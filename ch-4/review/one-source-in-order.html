Chapter 4 review · Drill

One Secondary Source, in the Method’s Order

You have a focusing question and a treatise open. Arrange the moves from first to last.

The order of operations (Put it in order)

Top is first.

  1. Match the source type to the question type before opening anything
    • Choosing the right secondary source for the question you are holding is the first efficiency the method buys you.
  2. Navigate by the table of contents and index to the section that addresses your question
    • You are looking for the discussion that answers your current question, not trying to absorb the whole treatise.
  3. Read until your question is answered, then stop
    • Stop the moment the question is answered; the rest of the section is later questions.
  4. Mine the footnotes for the primary authority that answers your question, and log the rest as leads
    • The footnotes are the bridge from the secondary source to the primary authority; each cited case is a candidate answer.
  5. Check the source’s currency: edition date, pocket part, “current through”
    • A secondary source can quietly fall out of date. Know how far to trust it before you verify.
  6. Open the primary authority the footnote named and verify the rule yourself
    • Your argument rests on the primary authority the source led you to, not on the source.
  7. Synthesize the verified answer into the annotated outline and take up the next question
    • The answer from primary authority goes into the outline; the question is marked answered on the map.

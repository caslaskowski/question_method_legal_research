Chapter 5 review · Drill

What Weight Does It Carry?

Same forum as Chapter 1: an Arizona trial court on a question of Arizona law. Sort each item by the weight it carries there.

Binding, persuasive, or not authority? (Quick check)

  • The majority opinion of the Arizona Supreme Court on the point Binding
    • A higher court in the same system, speaking for a majority. The law of the case and of the jurisdiction.
  • A headnote summarizing that opinion Not authority
    • An editor’s summary. Use it to navigate; never cite it. Only the opinion text carries precedential weight.
  • A dissent in that same case Persuasive
    • Not binding, but it can be powerful when its reasoning later becomes the majority view.
  • A concurrence that reaches the result by different reasoning Persuasive
    • Concurrences do not bind, but they identify arguments a future court may adopt.
  • An unpublished Arizona Court of Appeals memorandum decision Persuasive
    • Usually non-precedential, and whether it may be cited at all depends on the court’s rules. Check before you rely on it.
    • Unpublished opinions are usually not binding, and some courts restrict citing them. Check the rule.
  • A Ninth Circuit decision on a federal question raised in the case Persuasive
    • On Arizona law, no. On a federal question in a state court, still persuasive: the Ninth Circuit does not sit above Arizona’s courts. Only the U.S. Supreme Court binds all courts on federal law.
    • The Ninth Circuit binds federal district courts in Arizona, not Arizona’s state courts.
  • A citator’s red flag on the case you want to cite Not authority
    • A signal is a reminder to read the citing opinions, not a verdict. The opinions that generated the flag are the authority.
  • Dictum in a binding opinion, on a question the court was not deciding Persuasive
    • Dictum can be persuasive, and dictum from a high court can be very persuasive, but no court is obligated to follow it.

Use of AI in the Development of this Textbook

Generative AI was used in the development of this textbook. This section is my effort to disclose that use as transparently as possible, so you can be as informed as possible as you engage with the material. Please note, I reviewed and edited every chapter before publication, and I verified every statement of law and every citation against primary authority, the same rule Chapter 15 asks of you. Any errors that remain are mine. If you find one, tell me. That’s the beauty of a live, open-access textbook: I can fix it.

How did I Use GenAI? #

I used generative AI to help build this textbook and the accompanying interactive website and instructor edition, from early planning through final drafts. The ideas and frameworks are mine. The Question Method deviates sharply from the methodology most research textbooks describe. So, this textbook could not be created solely with a prompt and limited oversight. The book is built on a large body of my original work product: teaching notes, methodological descriptions, fully worked examples, chapter outlines, draft language for the core elements of each chapter, and samples of my writing so the drafting would sound like me. AI drafted from that foundation.I also matched the tool to the task rather than defaulting to the most powerful option: the smallest capable models for settled material and first drafts, larger models only where the work demanded them, for developing novel concepts at scale, for deep structural edits, and vibe coding to speed the development of the accompanying website.

Why Did I Use GenAI? #

I did not make the choice to utilize Generative AI lightly. I weighed the costs of using this tool, which I attempt to calculate below, against the value of a free, open textbook on a new method with a companion interactive website finished within a few months before the Fall 2026 semester. As with many libraries, our bandwidth is limited, and our desire to do the absolute best for the communities we serve is often bottlenecked by that bandwidth. As such, this tool gave me the ability to expand my internal teaching materials on this method into a full textbook that could better serve my students and hopefully students at other institutions.

Estimated Impact #

It was my intention from the beginning to limit the use of genAI to initial development. Nothing about reading, hosting, or updating this book will utilize another large language model. The accompanying interactive website might, but only if there are major technological issues that must be addressed within a timeframe too short for me to programmatically solve myself in a timely manner (e.g., a major accessibility concern). I can and intend to update the content and code myself as needed. Below are my best estimates of the environmental impact from the development of this textbook and accompanying resources. These numbers are in flux as people continue to do research, so I used the high end for my textbook and the median of ranges for the conventional textbook.

  • Using the high end of available estimates, developing this book and companion materials consumed roughly 12 kWh of energy (up to 12 kg of CO2, depending on the grid) and about 100 liters of water. Digital hosting adds an estimated 3 to 7 kWh and 1.3 to 3.1 kg of CO2 per gigabyte per year, and this book is a fraction of a gigabyte (I only know the size of the downloadable files, not the actual size of all code on the Pressbooks site).
  • For comparison, a conventional 400-page print textbook is estimated to consume 400 liters of water (over 5,000 at the high end), about 2 kg of timber (roughly a twentieth of a tree), and 2.7 to 7.5 kg of CO2 across its life cycle. This is per textbook.

What is harder to quantify, and deeply important to me, is the positive impact from students who pay nothing for their class materials, and, I hope, students who learn legal research better from the question method and more than one way to learn it.

The development costs for this textbook were paid once, for every reader. At least 40 students will be using this textbook this year between my fall and spring classes. To me, if this textbook can improve legal research comprehension, even among this small a cohort, the positive impact outweighs the negative externalities. Should it be adopted by others, which is the hope of any textbook writer, the cost per user shrinks, meaning the positive impact begins to exponentially outweigh the negative externalities.

| **4. Research Question** | What free speech protection applies to a school-sponsored student publication? |
| Question Type | Focusing |
| Method | Lead from the map: read the *Hazelwood* opinion. |
| Findings | A curricular student newspaper is not a public forum unless school officials have opened it, by policy or by practice, for indiscriminate use by the general public or some segment of it. The Court distinguished *Tinker*: tolerating student speech is a different question from affirmatively promoting it. Answers the question fully, for the governing rule. ## The Finished Annotated Outline

**Macro question:** Can the principal remove articles he does not like from the school-run student newspaper without violating the First Amendment?

1. Students retain free speech rights at school; restriction requires a showing of material and substantial disruption or collision with the rights of others. *Tinker* (1969).
    1. Student rights are not coextensive with adults’ rights; a school may bar lewd or vulgar speech inconsistent with its basic educational mission. *Fraser* (1986).
2. Educators may regulate the style and content of school-sponsored student expression when the regulation is reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical concerns; a curricular paper is not a public forum unless opened by policy or practice. *Hazelwood* (1988).
    1. A publication is considered “school-sponsored” if it is one the public might reasonably perceive to bear the school’s imprimatur: faculty-supervised, curricular, designed to teach. *Hazelwood*. \[Our facts: produced in a journalism class, supervised by a faculty advisor, published under the school’s name. Element met; no contrary facts in the file.\]
    2. Illustrative categories of a “legitimate pedagogical concern” include: learning the lesson, maturity-appropriate material, non-attribution of views to the school; a school may refuse to sponsor ungrammatical, poorly written, inadequately researched, biased, vulgar, or unsuitable speech. *Hazelwood*. \[Our facts: removal of articles the principal did not like, containing no profanity, threats, or illegality. The stated reasons map onto no recognized category.\]
3. When is a school publication a public forum “by policy or by practice”? Nonpublic unless opened for indiscriminate use by the public or a segment of it; courts examine written policy and actual practice. *Hazelwood*; *Dean*. \[Our facts, from the client call: content decisions made by the advisor and administration; no outside contributions accepted. The paper looks like a nonpublic forum. Contested point: expect the other side to argue a *Dean*-style limited public forum from practice.\]
4. Outer limits: the standard does not authorize viewpoint-based censorship or restrictions unconnected to any educational purpose. *Dean* (E.D. Mich. 2004). \[Slot: second on-point case at cite check. *Dean* is persuasive authority; confirm treatment.\]

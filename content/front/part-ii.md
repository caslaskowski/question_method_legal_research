Part I introduced the process. Part II provides the knowledge to apply everything you have learned to more complex and varied legal research processes. The sources get more specialized, the contexts more demanding, and the stakes more explicitly professional.

Chapter 10 comes first for a reason. Research quality is not a private matter between you and your grade. It is a professional obligation with rules attached, and lawyers are sanctioned for failing it. Reading the ethics chapter before the specialized materials sets the frame for everything that follows: the verification habits, the citator checks, the Research Log as a reviewable record. Chapter 10 will show you that these are the method’s own closing steps, performed with the rules of professional conduct in view.

Chapters 11 through 13 add sources most first-year courses never reach: dockets and court documents, legislative history, and the deeper administrative materials, including where agency interpretations stand after *Loper Bright*. Each follows the familiar template, so you will know exactly what to ask of it. Chapters 14 through 17 then shift from sources to contexts. Litigation, transactional, and compliance practice each bend the method to their own rhythms. Generative AI tools demand the cycle’s verification discipline more than any source before them. The NextGen bar exam tests the skills this book has been naming all along. And scholarship hands you a new kind of macro question, one where the thesis is the answer your paper exists to defend.

Unlike Part I, this part does not need to be read straight through. After Chapter 10, take the chapters as your coursework or your practice demands them.

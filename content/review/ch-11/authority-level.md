This chapter adds one clause to every log entry: the authority level, first. Sort each document.

::: {.try kind="sort" title="Binding, persuasive, or advisory?" bins="Binding regulation | Persuasive guidance | Advisory only | Binding within the agency"}
- 29 C.F.R. Part 1605, adopted after notice and comment **Binding regulation**
    - Promulgated with the force of law, within its statutory grant.
- The EEOC Compliance Manual section on religious accommodation **Persuasive guidance**
    - Influential, weighed under Skidmore in proportion to its reasoning, binding on no one.
- An agency FAQ page on its website **Persuasive guidance**
    - Guidance in its most informal dress. Still not law, however often regulated parties follow it.
- An SEC no-action letter to one company **Advisory only**
    - The staff’s view about one party’s facts. Advisory, and not even guidance for anyone else.
- A decision of the agency’s administrative law judge, affirmed by the agency head **Binding within the agency**
    - Precedent within the agency’s own process, subject to judicial review, and the most detailed application of the rule you will find.
- An informal opinion letter answering one employer’s question **Advisory only**
    - Advisory. Useful for predicting enforcement; never a rule.
- An interpretive rule published in the Federal Register as a notice **Persuasive guidance**
    - Publication in the Register does not make it a legislative rule. Without notice and comment it lacks the force of law.
    - Where it was published does not decide its weight; how it was adopted does.
- The CFR section the compliance manual interprets **Binding regulation**
    - The manual explains it; the section binds.
:::

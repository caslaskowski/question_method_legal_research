Same forum as the ranking drill: an Arizona trial court deciding a question of Arizona law. Sort each source. Remember the rule: an authority binds only when it comes from home, from your own jurisdiction’s law or from a court above yours in the same system.

::: {.try kind="sort" title="Does it bind this court?" bins="Binding | Persuasive"}
- The Arizona Constitution **Binding**
    - The supreme law of the jurisdiction. Everything else in Arizona yields to it.
- An Arizona statute on point **Binding**
    - The legislature’s enacted rule binds every Arizona court, so long as it does not conflict with the constitution.
- A regulation properly adopted by an Arizona agency under a statute **Binding**
    - A properly adopted regulation carries the force of law within its grant of authority.
- A decision of the Arizona Supreme Court **Binding**
    - A higher court in the same system. This is what stare decisis means in practice.
- A decision of the Ninth Circuit on a question of Arizona common law **Persuasive**
    - A federal appellate court does not sit above an Arizona court on Arizona law. On federal questions the picture changes.
    - Ask what question the court was answering. On Arizona law, Arizona’s own courts are the final word.
- A decision of another Arizona trial court **Persuasive**
    - Courts at the same level do not bind each other.
- The Restatement (Second) of Torts **Persuasive**
    - Secondary. Courts cite Restatements often, and a section the Arizona Supreme Court has adopted carries weight, but the authority then comes from the adopting opinion, not the Restatement.
- A New Mexico Supreme Court decision on drone privacy, where Arizona has no case **Persuasive**
    - When your jurisdiction is silent, a sister state’s decision is the best evidence of where your court may land. It still does not bind.
:::

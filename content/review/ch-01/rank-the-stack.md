An Arizona trial court is hearing a negligence claim, an area governed mostly by state common law. The parties have put six authorities in front of it. Arrange them from the one that controls most to the one that persuades least.

::: {.try kind="order" title="Which controls, which persuades?"}
Top is the most controlling; bottom is the least. Then check.

1. A U.S. Supreme Court decision interpreting the U.S. Constitution on a point raised in the case
    - All courts, state and federal, must follow the Supreme Court on the U.S. Constitution. Where it speaks, it sits at the top.
2. An Arizona Supreme Court decision on the duty at issue
    - The final word on Arizona law. Every Arizona court must follow it.
3. An Arizona Court of Appeals decision on the same duty
    - Binding on the trial courts below it, but it yields to the Arizona Supreme Court.
4. A federal district court sitting in Arizona, discussing Arizona negligence law
    - Persuasive only. A federal court is not above an Arizona court on a question of Arizona law; it takes its own cue from the Arizona Supreme Court.
5. A California Supreme Court decision on nearly identical facts
    - Persuasive. A sister state’s highest court can be very persuasive, especially when its framework resembles Arizona’s, but it binds no Arizona court.
6. A section of a well-regarded treatise on negligence
    - Secondary authority is always persuasive at most. Useful for finding and understanding the law, never for settling it.
:::

The cycle does not change; one step is added where these tools fail. Arrange the moves.

::: {.try kind="order" title="One question, one checkable answer"}
Top is first.

1. Write one narrow research question, with the role, the single task, and the format you want
    - Step 1 does most of the work. A compound question licenses a sprawling answer, and sprawl is where fabrications hide.
2. Decide whether this question type is one the tool handles well
    - Step 2 includes the instrument choice. Exploratory and focusing, yes; application, rarely.
3. Run the prompt and read the answer as a claim to test, not a finding
    - A confident tone is not an answer.
4. Retrieve every cited case, statute, and quotation and read it for the proposition claimed
    - The added verification step. Nothing enters your record unverified.
5. Log the verified answer, adding the system and the prompt to Source Consulted, and note whether it fully, partially, or not really answered
    - Step 3, with the one addition. If verifying cost as much as researching, that goes in Notes.
6. Identify the next question from the verified answer, and ask whether the tool is still earning its place
    - Steps 4 and 5, with the Checkpoint this chapter makes habitual.
:::

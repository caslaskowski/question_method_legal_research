Generative tools are strongest on exploratory and focusing questions and weakest on application questions. Sort each task.

::: {.try kind="sort" title="Which instrument?" bins="Generative AI, then verify | Terms-and-connectors search | Retrieve and read the primary source | Not a research task"}
- Orient me to the law of student speech in schools **Generative AI, then verify**
    - An exploratory question is where these tools help most. Every case it names still gets retrieved and read.
- Find every Ninth Circuit case applying Hazelwood where the school lost **Terms-and-connectors search**
    - An application question built on specific facts in decided cases: the tool’s failure mode. A precise Boolean search with a citator trace does it.
- Generate synonyms and search terms for “fired” **Generative AI, then verify**
    - Listed among the appropriate uses. The terms go into your search; nothing is cited.
- Confirm that the case the tool cited exists and says what it claimed **Retrieve and read the primary source**
    - The verification step. If you cannot find and read the source, the answer does not exist.
- State what the current law is on remote work as an ADA accommodation **Terms-and-connectors search**
    - Stating current law is on the inappropriate-without-verification list. Research it; the tool may summarize what you have already read and verified.
- Summarize an opinion you have already read and verified **Generative AI, then verify**
    - Appropriate, because you can check the summary against your own reading.
- Decide whether our client’s newspaper is school-sponsored **Not a research task**
    - That is the writing step, yours to make in a research pause. No tool and no database does it.
- Pull 597 U.S. 507 and read it for the proposition you need **Retrieve and read the primary source**
    - You hold a citation. Retrieve, don’t search, and read it yourself.
:::

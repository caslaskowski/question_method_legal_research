The single-source trap is a coverage failure: a governing authority type never reached, or reached only secondhand. A streak of same-type entries is not by itself a problem. Sort each log.

::: {.try kind="sort" title="Coverage failure, or fine?" bins="Single-source trap | Working as designed"}
- Five consecutive case entries on the elements of negligence, a common-law question **Working as designed**
    - The cases are where the rule lives. A long run of case entries is the research doing its job.
- Every statutory point in the log arrived through a case’s description of the statute; the code was never opened **Single-source trap**
    - The subtler form: the authority type appears, but only secondhand. Open the statute and read it with its definitions and exceptions.
- A workplace-safety question answered entirely from a treatise and two cases; the OSHA regulations were never consulted **Single-source trap**
    - A governing authority type is missing altogether. The regulation holds the operative requirement.
- Six application-question entries comparing decided cases to the client’s facts, after the statute and regulation were read directly **Working as designed**
    - Deep in application questions, case law is the right source, and the primary texts were already read.
- A regulatory point known only from an agency FAQ page; the CFR section itself unread **Single-source trap**
    - Guidance summarizes. The operative CFR text, and the sections around it, bind your client.
- A local sign ordinance that governs the client’s dispute is nowhere in the log **Single-source trap**
    - The research map should have listed every authority type in play. The ordinance was never reached.
- Three secondary-source entries at the start of an unfamiliar project, then a move to the statute **Working as designed**
    - Orientation first, then primary authority. That is the method’s own sequence.
:::

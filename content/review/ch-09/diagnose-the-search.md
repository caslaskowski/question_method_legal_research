What sets competent researchers apart is diagnosing the problem and applying a specific correction. Sort each symptom by its first fix.

::: {.try kind="sort" title="What is the first move?" bins="Broaden with synonyms and OR | Remove or loosen proximity | Narrow with proximity or filters | Switch source type | Conclude no law, with documentation"}
- Zero results on a precise Boolean search in a well-litigated area **Broaden with synonyms and OR**
    - Your terms are probably too specific. Add synonyms, or reduce the number of terms, before you conclude anything.
- Zero results after /3 proximity; AND alone returns a manageable set **Remove or loosen proximity**
    - The proximity constraint is excluding the documents you want. Loosen it to /s or /p.
- Four thousand results on an ADA accommodation search **Narrow with proximity or filters**
    - Too broad. Set the jurisdiction and source-type filters, add proximity, or require substantive treatment with ATLEAST.
- Twelve case searches and still no rule, on a question that turns out to be governed by a regulation **Switch source type**
    - The single-source trap from Chapter 8. Return to a secondary source or the annotated code and let it point you to the CFR.
- Alternative terms, loosened proximity, persuasive jurisdictions, and secondary sources all come up empty **Conclude no law, with documentation**
    - Only after exhausting the alternatives is “no law” defensible, and the searches you ran are the documentation.
- Every result discusses the right doctrine but a different setting than your client’s **Narrow with proximity or filters**
    - The precision trap. Require the setting’s terms near the rule’s terms, so the results answer your question rather than the general topic.
:::

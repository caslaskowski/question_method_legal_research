The mapping runs from breadth to depth. Sort each situation into the source type that answers it best.

::: {.try kind="sort" title="Match the source to the question" bins="Encyclopedia | Treatise or hornbook | ALR, Restatement, or survey | Practice guide | Law review"}
- You have never heard of the area and need vocabulary and a frame **Encyclopedia**
    - A no-clue question. Am. Jur. 2d, C.J.S., or your state’s encyclopedia gives breadth, not depth.
- You know the area exists and need its full doctrinal structure, with the leading cases **Treatise or hornbook**
    - Foundational questions want the internal logic of a doctrine and the authorities behind it.
- You need to know how courts in many jurisdictions have treated one narrow issue **ALR, Restatement, or survey**
    - A survey question. An ALR annotation collects the cases from many jurisdictions on exactly that issue.
- You are drafting the papers and need the forms, checklists, and local practice **Practice guide**
    - Practice guides go past doctrine to the steps of representing a client.
- Primary authority leaves a real grey area and you need the arguments on each side **Law review**
    - Scholarly articles analyze contested questions and propose answers; use them when the primary law runs out.
- You need the judicial definitions courts have given a specific term **Treatise or hornbook**
    - Close, but the chapter’s answer is Words & Phrases, which collects judicial definitions; a treatise is the next-best general source. Either way, the definition becomes authoritative only in the primary source.
    - The chapter names Words & Phrases for judicial definitions; of the bins offered, the treatise comes closest.
- You want same-day commentary on a Supreme Court opinion released this morning **Law review**
    - Trick: a blawg such as SCOTUSblog covers same-day developments; a law review will not for months. Of the bins here, law review is the nearest, but verify anything a blog says against the opinion itself.
    - For recent developments the chapter points to blawgs, with verification against primary sources.
:::

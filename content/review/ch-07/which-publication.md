Each publication answers a different question about the same rule: what was proposed, what is codified, what is current today.

::: {.try kind="sort" title="Where to look" bins="Federal Register | CFR (annual) | eCFR | LSA"}
- The agency’s explanation of why it wrote the rule the way it did **Federal Register**
    - The preamble to the final rule lives in the Federal Register, not in the codified text.
- The most current text of 29 C.F.R. § 1605.2, as of today **eCFR**
    - Updated daily, free, with a note showing the date through which each title is current.
- You are working from last year’s printed volume and need to catch amendments since **LSA**
    - The List of CFR Sections Affected bridges the annual edition and today.
- A proposed rule published last week and open for comment **Federal Register**
    - Proposed rules, interim rules, and notices are the Federal Register’s daily business.
- The official codified text as it stood on the annual revision date **CFR (annual)**
    - The annual edition is the official codification, organized in fifty titles by subject.
- Public comments stakeholders filed on the proposal **Federal Register**
    - Trick: comments live on Regulations.gov in the rulemaking docket; the Federal Register notice is where you find the docket number. Of the bins here, the Register is the entry point.
    - Comments sit in the docket on Regulations.gov; the Federal Register notice points you to it.
:::

Scholarship gives the method a wider field and a longer clock. Arrange the stages.

::: {.try kind="order" title="From inventory to submission"}
Top is first.

1. Harvest the questions your reading left open into a question inventory
    - A well-fed inventory means you may never face the blank page.
2. Pick a candidate and test it: supportable, open or fixable, worth answering, livable
    - The four things to ask of a topic.
3. Run the one-hour cursory preemption check
    - Does your argument already exist? Know before the outline exists.
4. Map the scholarly landscape, reaching past the law reviews where the issue does
    - Positions, agreements, disagreements, gaps.
5. Run the full preemption protocol when the thesis firms up, and write the contribution sentence
    - Search the claims, read for overlap, differentiate or pivot.
6. Draft the outline in question form and research each section to closure
    - Framework, problem, why it fails, the fix: one question at a time.
7. Run the final preemption sweep before submission
    - A preempting article can land while you are writing.
:::

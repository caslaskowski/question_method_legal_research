You have seen these documents in the order Congress creates them. Now arrange them by interpretive weight, most to least.

::: {.try kind="order" title="Most weight to least"}
Top carries the most weight.

1. The conference report reconciling the House and Senate versions
    - High. Both chambers’ final agreement on disputed language; often the definitive source on compromised text.
2. The committee report on the bill
    - High. The drafting committee’s collective explanation; courts cite committee reports frequently.
3. Bill versions showing “knowingly” added late in the process
    - Moderate. Evidence of how language evolved, and of intent to require a mental state.
4. A floor statement by the bill’s sponsor, actually spoken
    - Variable, but the sponsor’s spoken statement is the strongest kind of floor evidence.
5. Hearing testimony from an industry witness
    - Moderate to low. Informative, but one viewpoint, not legislative consensus.
6. A statement a member inserted into the Congressional Record without speaking it
    - Reduced weight. Inserted statements were never heard by the chamber.
7. The presidential signing statement
    - Low to moderate. The executive’s perspective, not legislative intent; the President is not a legislator.
:::

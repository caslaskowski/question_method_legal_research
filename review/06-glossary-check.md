# Glossary check against the core thesis

The author's glossary of about 120 terms was checked against what the book commits to: there is no one good research path; application questions in research ask how courts or other authorities have applied the rule to facts in decided matters, while applying the rule to the client's facts is a writing step; the process pauses (Quick Check, Checkpoint, research pause, closing-out protocol); the three tools; and the chain of research down one element before moving sideways. The merged glossary has 123 entries: the author's list (corrected where noted), plus sixteen entries the list lacked that chapters define (the five NextGen skills, the three Model Rule entries, closed universe, FOIA request, frequency operator, opinion letter, the three practice-setting closures, treaty).

## Definitions corrected, and why

| Term | What the list said | What it says now | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| application question | "how a rule operates on specific facts" | how courts or other authorities have applied a rule to facts in decided matters; applying it to the client's facts is a writing step | Chapter 3's distinction between the research form and the writing form of an application question |
| fact-finding application | locating decisions with facts like the client's | locating those decisions and recording how the courts applied the rule | the answer is the decided application, not the match |
| constructive application | building the rule from cases and reasoning by analogy | same, with the argument that the rule reaches the client's facts placed in the writing step | same distinction |
| dispositive facts | "facts used in the reasoning to apply the rule to the current case" | the facts the decision turned on; comparing them with the client's facts is the writing step | "current case" read as the client's; the comparison is writing |
| research map | "the pre-search sketch of which authority types could govern an issue" | the running list of questions still to answer, built over time, with leads beside each | Chapter 3 and the author's quick reference define the map as the question list; the authority-type sketch is the research strategy |
| Research Log | six items listed | the seven scaffold fields, and "no single right format" | Chapter 3 and the log template |
| closing-out protocol | five items including "check secondary-source currency" | the six items of the author's checklist | the checklist governs; secondary-source currency is part of updating every authority |
| single-source trap | a long run of same-type log entries | a coverage failure; a run of same-type entries is not by itself the trap | Chapter 8 says so in terms |
| Checkpoint | as listed | adds the contrast: the Checkpoint asks whether the research is working, the Quick Check whether you are | the two pauses are easy to conflate |
| Quick Check | missing | added | it is a named step of the cycle |
| Question Method | write, research, record, next question | adds naming the type, mapping what the source raised, and both pauses | the cycle as Chapter 3 now states it |
| definitional question | dictionary first, then statute or case law "when precision is critical" | statute's or regulation's definitions section first, then case law, for a binding definition; dictionary for a working or persuasive one | Chapter 6 and the quick reference: statute first, cases only after the text is silent |
| focusing question | "the move from secondary to primary authority" | answered from the statute, regulation, or case that states the rule and its sub-rules; usually that move | the source, not only the move |
| element | "each one becomes its own research question" | each one heads its own chain of research questions, run down through its sub-issues before moving sideways | the chain of research |
| holding | as listed | adds: when the court's issue matches yours, the holding is the answer you record | Appendix B |
| annotated outline | as listed | adds: the R and E of CREAC | the quick reference |
| majority opinion | "the one that states the law of the case" | the one that states the court's holding and carries precedential weight | "law of the case" is a term of art for a different doctrine; Chapter 5's sentence changed to match |
| proximity connector | /s and /p require the same sentence or paragraph | shorthand for default word distances the platforms do not publish | the author's own ruling, recorded in EDITING-NOTES; Chapter 9's sentence tightened to match |
| natural language searching | as listed | adds that the database does the ranking | the point Chapter 2 makes about the trade-off |
| practical closure, constitution, legislative intent, hallucination, citator | grammar | repaired, meaning kept | |

Everything else in the author's list is used as written. Chapter tags were kept from the existing entries and, for the 37 new terms, set to the first chapter or appendix that uses the term. "Precision trap" is in the glossary but no chapter uses the phrase yet; the idea appears in Chapter 3's "false hit" checkpoint.

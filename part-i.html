Part I: Foundational Legal Research Topics

Part I is the complete foundation: everything you need to complete most basic research projects from first question to documented closure. Chapter 1 maps where the law lives, because you cannot decide where to look until you know what is out there. Chapter 2 introduces the platforms and search mechanics you will use every working day. Then Chapter 3 introduces the Question Method itself, the engine the rest of this book runs on. Read those three in order. Everything after them assumes you have.

Chapters 4 through 7 each take up one major source type: secondary sources, case law, statutes, and regulations. They share a deliberate template. Each shows you how the source fits the research cycle, what kinds of questions it answers, how it is made and what that means for your research, and how to search it. The repetition is the point. By the fourth pass, the questions you ask of a new source will start to feel automatic, and that is the habit forming.

Chapters 8 and 9 put the pieces together. Real projects rarely live in one source; Chapter 8 shows you how statutes, regulations, and cases interlock, and how to run the cycle across all of them at once. Chapter 9 teaches the endgame: precision searching once your questions have narrowed, and the closing-out protocol that lets you say, with a documented basis, that the research is done. Between the macro question you write down at the start and the closure you document at the end, every move in this part is one you will use for the rest of your career.

A word on how to work through it. The chapters include drills, and Appendix E holds the model walkthroughs, kept apart so you cannot read an answer by accident. Attempt every drill before you look. The method only becomes yours through repetition, and Part I is where you get them. Do the reps.
